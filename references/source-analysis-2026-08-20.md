### Observed mechanics

- A strong cover uses a culturally recognizable person or scene plus a long,
  specific tension.
- The carousel moves through problem, evidence, distinction, mechanism,
  application, checklist, and offer rather than presenting isolated tips.
- Dark image-led slides alternate with light editorial explanation, diagrams,
  screenshots, and evidence panels. The alternation creates rhythm without
  changing the argument.
- Real or presented proof appears as charts, post screenshots, examples, or
  stated business results.
- Acquisition pieces often end in a lead magnet or product triggered by a
  comment keyword and automated DM.

### Inference

The repeatable advantage is not the cover style. It is the conversion of an
already interesting conversation into a niche-specific mechanism the reader can
apply. The commercial offer feels earned when it resolves the same mechanism.

### Signature not to reproduce

- celebrity/creator hero image on dark color grading;
- very heavy condensed white headline with orange/red emphasis;
- “Powered by Content Machine” and dense microheader treatment;
- hook shells such as “what X teaches about Y”, “the death of X”, or
  “decoding X”;
- a near-identical problem → models → checklist → comment-keyword offer sequence.

### Observed content structures

| Structure | Sequence |
| --- | --- |
| Accumulated recognition | Repeated phrase across micro-pains → brand resolution |
| Service qualification | Cover → one service and expectation per slide → contact CTA |
| Case | Number headline → challenge → mechanism → artifacts → metric ladder → testimonial/next step |
| Education | Simple premise → paired translations/examples → follow/save action |
| Trend critique | Provocation → soften absolute claim → relevance criteria → decision rule |
| Positioning | Exclusion → method → client obligations → moderate promise |

### Post-level reading

| Post | Observed argument and construction | What Hanna retains |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Existe paz” | Five-slide accumulation of recognizable agency pains under one repeated phrase, resolved by the brand on the last slide | Accumulate concrete micro-symptoms before resolving a commercial tension; never reuse the phrase or photographic treatment |
| ExpoAgroPec case | Result headline → challenge → intermediate indicators → digital as one pillar → execution artifacts → final metric board | Contextualize a result with mechanism, artifacts, metric level, and attribution limits instead of isolating a large number |
| “Troque isso por isso” | Technical terms translated into everyday language in repeated blocks, followed by a follow action | Build saveable education through structured translation when the audience genuinely needs the vocabulary |
| Urban visibility static | One short claim placed as an apparent street poster; the image carries the situation | Let a static piece name one costly blind spot, but use Datta's own composition rather than the street-poster device |
| Services | One service per slide, each paired with expectations and conditions; contact appears only after the scope is clear | Explain what the work requires and qualify fit instead of listing deliverables without operational consequences |
| Trend critique | Provocation → qualification of the absolute claim → attention/relevance criteria → business decision | Challenge a fashionable tactic conditionally, then supply a selection rule |
| Price objection static | A provocative question reframes low price as a possible source of agency disappointment | Address an objection by exposing its decision logic, without copying the regional teasing tone |
| Positioning | Explicit exclusion → business-first method → client information obligations → moderate promise | Qualify the buyer by process requirements and collaboration, not by unsupported superiority |

Observed case metrics remain claims of the publication and were not independently
verified. Apparent outdoor, screen, poster, or event placements remain contextual
artifacts or mockups unless a separate source proves real placement.

### Observed visual system

- Heavy grotesque uppercase, force-justified blocks, and a high-contrast italic
  editorial voice.
- Acid lime, black/charcoal, off-white paper, grain, halftone, tape, and torn or
  wrinkled texture.
- Photographs work as context, texture, proof, or mockup rather than polished
  portraits.
- Posters, billboards, transit panels, computers, phones, and paper turn copy
  into an apparent physical media object.
- Client cases may let the client's identity dominate while Medre keeps its
  narrative and type roles.

### Inference

The transferable mechanism is a commercially uncomfortable truth organized in
steps, supported by contextual artifacts and proof proportional to the claim.
The acid street-poster treatment is their signature, not the mechanism.

### Signature not to reproduce

- acid lime + wrinkled paper + rough black + expanded uppercase + italic serif;
- recurring urban poster, billboard, or transit mockups with provocative copy;
- “Existe paz em...”, “não somos para qualquer empresa”, “sobrinho”,
  “chororô”, or their regional `tu` balance of teasing and pressure;
- any client, number, material, or layout from the ExpoAgroPec case.

## Image-origin finding

The feed does not establish whether the strongest images came from stock,
commissioned photography, archives, mockups, or generative AI. The reasonable
classification is mixed and inconclusive:

- client/event materials appear tied to real project contexts, but authorship
  and license are not visible;
- public figures, film scenes, and cultural images appear editorial, but the
  inspected captions do not consistently document rights;
- urban placements and screens may be digital mockups rather than real media;
- no reliable public marker proves or disproves generative AI use.

Hanna therefore uses provenance rather than visual guessing.
