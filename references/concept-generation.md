# Concept generation

A brief that goes straight to layout produces the first idea, and the first idea
is almost always the category's idea. This reference is the divergence step that
runs between the strategy and the art direction: how to turn a business truth
into several genuinely different visual ideas, and how to choose one on evidence
rather than taste.

Run it for every static, carousel, and ad. It costs one paragraph of thinking
and it is the difference between a competent piece and one worth stopping for.

## From business truth to visible idea

Answer the ladder in order. Do not skip to the last rung.

```text
1. TRUTH        What is factually true about this operation?
2. BREAK        Where does it break, and what does the break cost?
3. WHO          Who is standing there when it breaks? What are they doing?
4. WHERE        In what physical place, screen, document, or object is it visible?
5. OBJECT       What single thing could hold the whole idea?
6. SEEN         What will the audience literally see in frame one?
```

Rung 6 must be describable to a photographer or an illustrator without using
the word "concept". If the sentence needs the argument attached to make sense,
the idea is not visual yet — it is a caption wearing a costume.

Weak rung 6: "an image representing data-driven decisions."
Usable rung 6: "a printed report on a meeting table with every row highlighted
in a different colour, so nothing is highlighted."

## Concept operators

Generate options by applying operators, not by asking for "something creative".
Each operator is a transformation you run on the ladder's rungs. Reach for the
ones the topic has not already exhausted.

| Operator | Transformation | Datta-shaped example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Materialization | Give the invisible a physical body, weight, or cost | An unattributed lead as a paper ticket nobody picks up |
| Scale shift | Make the small enormous, or the enormous pocket-sized | One duplicated event, printed at poster size |
| Magnified evidence | Get close enough that the artifact becomes a landscape | Macro of a UTM string breaking mid-word |
| Same frame, changed state | Hold the camera still and change one variable | The same desk at 9:02 and at 17:40, one ticket unmoved |
| Substitution | Keep a familiar scene, replace one element with the topic | A clinic queue where each chair holds a form instead of a person |
| Exploded view | Take a system apart and lay the parts out in order | The tracking chain as separated physical components |
| Displaced viewpoint | Shoot from where no one normally looks | The dashboard from behind the screen, seen by the person it never reaches |
| Literalization | Take an industry phrase at its word | "Perder lead" as an object actually lost under furniture |
| Absence | Show the hole rather than the thing | A metrics board with one column deliberately removed |
| Displaced artifact | Put the subject in a period or material where it doesn't belong | A CAC calculation as a handwritten ledger entry |
| Instrumentation | Add a measuring device to an ordinary scene | Brackets and a time label on a photograph of a waiting room |
| Sequence break | Set a pattern in three beats, break it in the fourth | Four identical reports, the fourth annotated |

Two operators can combine. Three usually collapse into noise.

## Three territories, then one

Before any layout decision, write three concept territories. They must differ in
kind, not in decoration. Three colour variations of the same picture is one
territory.

```text
TERRITORY A — [operator] — [what the audience sees in one sentence]
  Strength:
  Risk:
TERRITORY B — [different operator] — [what the audience sees]
  Strength:
  Risk:
TERRITORY C — [different operator] — [what the audience sees]
  Strength:
  Risk:
SELECTED: [A | B | C] because [reason tied to the score below]
```

Territories are different in kind when they change the *class* of what appears:
a scene versus an artifact versus a system versus an absence. If all three
sentences could illustrate each other's copy, generate again.

## Selection score

Score each territory 1–3. Select on the total, then sanity-check the floors.

| Criterion | 1 | 3 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Truth | Needs a claim you cannot support | Holds up with what is actually known |
| Specificity | Fits any agency in any niche | Only works for this operation |
| Three-second read | Needs the caption to land | Lands from the image alone |
| Producibility | Needs assets you cannot license or make | Producible with available routes |
| Distinction | Recognisable from category shorthand | Nobody in this feed has shown this |

Floors that override the total:

- Truth below 3 is disqualifying, not a trade-off.
- Producibility of 1 is disqualifying for a piece requested as final; it may
  survive as a documented blocker in a draft.
- Distinction of 1 disqualifies even a high total. A safe, invisible piece has
  already failed its only job.

## Kill tests

Run these on the selected territory before art direction.

- **Niche swap.** Replace the industry with an unrelated one. If only the nouns
  change and the picture still works, the concept is generic — recompose.
- **Seen it.** Name where you have seen this exact image before. If you can, it
  is category shorthand: handshake, rising arrow, glowing brain, hooded hacker,
  floating dashboard, team high-five, magnifying glass over graph.
- **Removal.** Delete the visual idea and leave the headline. If the piece loses
  nothing, the visual is decoration and the concept never existed.
- **Explanation.** If the metaphor needs a sentence to unlock, it is a puzzle,
  not a hook. A metaphor may reward a second look; it may not require one.
- **Flattery.** If the idea's only move is telling the reader they are smarter
  than their competitors, it is not an insight.

## Concept card

Carry this into art direction. It is the bridge between this reference and
[Art direction and images](art-direction-and-images.md).

```text
CONCEPT
- Operator(s) used:
- What the audience sees:
- What it makes understandable that copy alone cannot:
- Why it is true:
- Where it recurs or transforms across the sequence:
- Kill tests: niche swap / seen it / removal / explanation / flattery — result
```

## Common mistakes

- Producing one territory and calling the selection step done.
- Three territories that are the same picture in three palettes.
- Choosing the most beautiful territory over the most true one.
- A metaphor that flatters the brand rather than exposing the reader's problem.
- Stacking operators until the image needs a legend.
- Treating "abstract geometry" as a territory. It is a fallback, not an idea.
- Letting the concept die at layout: the selected operator must still be
  identifiable in the finished artwork.
